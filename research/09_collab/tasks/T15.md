## What to do
1. For every item, find concrete exchanges in `exchanges.jsonl` that produced it. Use the checker's semantics; you may run `conformance.py` on the run yourself.
2. Locate the responsible code, the log evidence, and the spec element.
3. Decide per the codebook:
   - L1 and L2.
   - Confidence 1–3.
   - Evidence types: E-CODE-INTENT, E-LOG, E-OBS, etc.
   - Evidence locators: `file:line`, exchange line numbers in `exchanges.jsonl`, `compose.log` line numbers.
   - A rationale of 30 words or fewer.
4. Where the codebook has no fitting L2, propose a new code in a `proposed_L2` column and explain it in the report. Do not force-fit.
5. Additionally group items into **root causes**: a `root_cause_id` (RC01…) with a one-line description. Many fingerprints may share one cause.
6. Flag items where you believe SPEC and IMPL are **both** defective (BOTH candidates), with the evidence for each side.
